## 2. Key Corrections

### 1. Spend time + -ing

**Original:**

> Today I spent a lot of time to catch up with my contacts of my team.

**More natural:**

> Today, I spent a lot of time catching up on my team's context.

Use `spend time + -ing`. Also, `catch up on` works well for information, developments, or work you missed.

### 2. Make better use of our resources

**Original:**

> I summarized all the tasks that our team had to do in this quarter, and we organize the tasks for better effort efficiency.

**More natural:**

> I summarized all the tasks our team needs to work on this quarter and reorganized them to make better use of our resources.

`effort efficiency` is understandable but unnatural. In management contexts, `make better use of our resources` is much more idiomatic.

### 3. After getting off

**Original:**

> Let me get back to conversation after getting off subway.

**Correction:**

> Let me get back to our conversation after getting off the subway.

**More natural:**

> Let me get back to our conversation after I get off the subway.

`after getting off` is grammatically correct. `after + -ing` works naturally when its implied subject is clear. In this sentence, `after I get off the subway` is clearer and more natural.

### 4. Hold back

**Original:**

> I spend a lot of energy to restrict my expression and characteristics.

**More natural:**

> I spend a lot of energy holding back my natural reactions and expressions.

`hold back` means to restrain yourself from saying, showing, or doing something.

### 5. Advance in my career

**Original:**

> If I want to stay in this company and want to promote it to a better position, then I have to decide.

**More natural:**

> If I want to stay at this company and move into a higher position, I have to make a decision.

Another natural option:

> If I want to stay at this company and advance in my career, I need to make a decision.

`promote` usually needs a person as its object:

> The company promoted me.

For your own career progression, use:

- get promoted
- move into a higher position
- advance in my career

## 4. Today's Focus

### Pattern: `spend + time/energy + -ing`

Today I used two similar constructions:

> I spent a lot of time **to catch up**...

> I spend a lot of energy **to restrict**...

The natural pattern is:

> **spend + time/energy + -ing**

Examples:

> I spent a lot of time **catching up**.

> I spend a lot of energy **holding myself back**.

This is a useful pattern to practice because it appeared naturally more than once during today's conversation.

### Tendency vs. Character vs. Characteristic

**tendency** = 성향 / 경향

> I have a tendency to overthink things.

**character** = 성격 / 인품 as a broader concept

In everyday American English, `personality` is often more natural when you simply mean **성격**.

**characteristic** = 특징 / 특성

> Patience is an important characteristic of a good manager.

For “내 성격이 문제인 것 같다,” a natural expression is:

> I think some aspects of my personality might be causing problems.
